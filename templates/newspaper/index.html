Your mastery
deserves infrastructure
that matches it.

One person, one platform, no duct tape. Why the agencies won't build it, why the platforms won't sell it, and why 170 practitioners have already stopped renting from landlords who read their mail.

There is a crisis in the practice economy, and it is entirely made of login screens. The modern healer, teacher, or retreat-holder operates on roughly seven platforms at once: a website they don't own, a course engine that rents them their students, a booking tool that takes a surcharge per session, an email provider that calls itself an audience, a community platform sitting on someone else's servers, a link-in-bio that monetizes their own traffic, and a Stripe account wired to all of the above with rubber bands. None of it speaks. All of it sends a bill. Together, they cost about $346 a month and produce a digital presence that matches none of the depth of the actual work.

Into this crowded, glitching market comes a different kind of offer. Not a platform. Not an agency. A single engineer in the village of Mazunte, Oaxaca, working from a hammock with a satellite dish and a cup of coffee, who builds the whole thing for one person at a time. His name is Jordi Amat. He has shipped around 170 of them. The first version typically arrives at the client's inbox within forty-eight hours — not as a wireframe, not as a proposal, but as an actual working site with their photos, their copy, their bookings live, and their payments open.

The economics of this are made possible by two things. The first is a decade of production engineering in startups you may have heard of, which means the code is not the kind that falls over in production. The second is the disciplined use of AI tools — not the lazy hallucinating kind, but the kind that, when paired with senior judgment, moves about five times faster than a senior engineer alone. The result is that Astral Integration charges roughly a fifth of what an equivalent agency quotes, and delivers in about a twentieth of the time, without compromising either the craft or the longevity of the build.

Clients include Deva Premal & Miten (music), Sacred Counsel (ceremony), Shiv Energetics (reiki academy), Gurmukh Kaur (yoga legacy), and Mariana Harpreet (multi-brand studio). All own every line of code from the first commit. Full story continues on p. 2.
