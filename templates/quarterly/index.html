№ 01 · From the Editor

A short letter
from Mazunte.

It has been eleven years since I started building digital infrastructure for practitioners — the teachers, healers, retreat-holders and ceremony-keepers who are, in my view, doing the most original and under-supported work in the world. Each year I build a little faster and I charge a little less, which is either the opposite of every business course I've ever skipped, or the natural shape of a trade that is finally in balance.

This quarterly is a small indulgence. It is my fourth issue. Inside, you will find a case study of the platform I built with Sacred Counsel (a twenty-year ceremony center that had been running on five disconnected tools); a photo essay from the desk where it all happens; a manifesto on why I think infrastructure is a form of care; and — tucked at the back — a rate card and a way to write to me.

The through-line, if there is one, is this: your digital presence should match the depth of your practice. Not slick. Not wellness-brand. Not VC-funded. Built with care, by one person, for one other person, and shipped fast enough to feel like respect. If that resonates, read on. If not, the classifieds on page 28 are also excellent.

Editor & builder, Astral Integration
— Jordi Amat
№ 02 · Feature Article · Case Study

How Sacred Counsel
replaced seven platforms
in twelve days.

A twenty-year ceremony center arrives with a linktree, five revenue streams, and a broken payment flow. What happens when the agency proposal is zero pages long.

Sacred Counsel arrived the way most of my clients arrive: through a voice memo. Teresa, the founder, had recorded it at 11:47 on a Sunday night, sitting on a veranda in Valle de Bravo. Twenty years of ceremony. Five revenue streams — a retreat program, a teacher training, a members' circle, a small publishing line, and private sessions — all quietly bleeding out through a patchwork of rented tools.

Her stack was familiar. A WordPress site from 2017 that loaded at 6.4 seconds on mobile. Bookings on Calendly. Payments through an old Stripe link half the team had forgotten about. A mailing list on Mailchimp's free tier, capped. An Instagram that was essentially the website. And a linktree serving as the "home." What she needed was not better versions of those seven tools. What she needed was one platform.

“She sent the mess.
Twelve days later
we shipped.”

We talked for thirty minutes on the first call. She sent me her workbook. Four days later she received a link. Not a mockup — the real site, with her copy, her photography, the first three retreats already open for booking, and the payment flow live. On day seven the course platform for her Ceremony Keeper training was live. On day nine we imported the mailing list, wired welcome automations in her voice, and opened the members' circle. On day twelve we migrated her domain and sent the announcement email.

The economics: a fixed fee at the Schools tier ($5,000), a monthly partnership at $750/mo which replaces roughly $430/mo in tool rentals plus the 7% Eventbrite was taking on a hundred tickets a quarter. Within six months the new platform had paid for itself. Within the first year, she opened her first eight-person one-to-one mentorship which could not have existed in the old stack. None of this is unusual for my practice — it is the thing the old architecture was preventing.
